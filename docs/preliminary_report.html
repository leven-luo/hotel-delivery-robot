2. Project description

We build a hotel delivery robot that carries a tall cup on an actively tilting two-axis tray and must reach a guest room from the lobby as fast as possible without tipping the cup or colliding with people. The policy jointly controls forward speed, tray pitch and tray roll. Tilting the tray toward the effective gravity vector g − a lets the robot brake and turn harder than a fixed tray would allow.

The hotel map (corridors, doors, turns, intersections) is known, but whether a person steps out of a door or a blind corner is not: such events occur with location-dependent probabilities, and the policy only receives what its sensors could observe at that moment. Bumps and slippery patches add disturbances. The task combines optimal control (speed and tray commands jointly determine travel time and tipping risk along the whole trajectory) with reinforcement learning (acting under partial observability, e.g. learning where to slow down before doors without being told whether someone is behind them).

3. Tools

Simulator — written by us (Python/NumPy, Gymnasium API). We use our own lightweight simulator rather than MuJoCo/PyBullet because it runs thousands of episodes per minute on a CPU and gives exact control over the quantities that matter here (actuator delays, event probabilities, partial observability). The contact between cup and tray only needs a tipping/sliding criterion, not a full contact solver. It models:

  • Car: differential drive with first-order motor lag on speed and yaw rate, acceleration limits, wheel slip on wet patches, and vertical/pitch excitation from bumps through a second-order suspension.
  • Tray: two servos with pure delay, first-order lag, rate limit and ±15° angle limit; chassis tilt from acceleration and bumps.
  • Cup (rigid cylinder, base radius r, center-of-mass height h, friction μ). Let f = g − a be the specific force expressed in the tray frame, with components f∥ (in the tray plane) and f⊥ (normal to it). The cup tips if |f∥|/f⊥ > r/h for longer than a short dwell time, and slides if it exceeds μ. The tipping margin m = 1 − (|f∥|/f⊥)/(r/h) is used for logging and reward shaping. This criterion also tells us which cup to buy: r/h must make the cup tip within the car's reachable accelerations (≈1.5–3 m/s²).
  • Hotel map: a graph of corridor segments with doors, turns and intersections. Pedestrians appear at doors and blind corners with location-dependent probabilities, and are visible only within the forward sensing range once not occluded.

Existing code we reuse: Stable-Baselines3 and sb3-contrib (SAC, PPO, RecurrentPPO) on PyTorch; Gymnasium; CasADi with the IPOPT solver for MPC; SciPy (discrete Riccati equation) for LQR. Written by us: the simulator and environment, all baselines (feedforward, LQR and MPC formulations), training and evaluation scripts, ESP32 firmware and the dual-IMU tip detector.

Hardware (planned). Waveshare UGV02 chassis (ESP32, 9-axis IMU, wheel encoders, 4 kg payload); a 3D-printed two-axis gimbal (fixed bracket, outer frame, food tray) with a DS3218 servo on one side of each axis and a 625ZZ bearing on the other; an ESP32 tray controller running the policy; IMUs on the tray and inside the cup (ESP-NOW) to detect tipping; a forward ToF sensor for bumps.

4. Algorithms

MDP. Control step 0.05 s (20 Hz), discount γ = 0.99. Episode ends on arrival, tipping, collision or timeout.

Reward rt−ct·Δt − wu‖at − at−1‖² − wm·max(0, m0 − mt); +Rgoal on arrival; −Rtip on tipping; −Rcol on collision. Weights are tuned so that a failure costs more than any realistic time saving.

Optimal-control baselines.

  • Fixed tray with a constant speed and with a hand-tuned "slow near doors" profile (lower bounds).
  • Gravity-aligned feedforward (already implemented): the tray target is the angle that aligns the cup with g − a, θ* = atan2(a, g + az) per axis. The acceleration is predicted 0.12 s ahead by passing the planner's commands through the motor-lag model, to compensate servo lag.
  • LQR tracking: linearize servo and chassis-tilt dynamics. The state is the tray-angle error e = θ − θ* with its rate and the servo's internal states. Minimize Σ eᵀQe + uᵀRu, with the gain from the discrete Riccati equation, on top of the feedforward. This keeps the cup axis aligned with g − a despite servo lag.
  • MPC (receding horizon, N = 20 steps = 2 s, CasADi/IPOPT, warm-started) over speed and tray angles. Cost: −progress + w‖Δu‖². Constraints: car and servo dynamics and limits; no-tip |f∥|/f⊥ ≤ κ·r/h with safety factor κ < 1; and at every door or blind corner within the horizon, v ≤ √(2·abrake·dvisible), i.e. the robot can always stop for a worst-case pedestrian. Safe by construction but conservative, which is the gap RL should close.

Reinforcement learning.

  • SAC: off-policy and sample-efficient for continuous actions; used with stacked observations.
  • PPO / RecurrentPPO (LSTM): handles partial observability through memory.
  • Residual RL for the tray: θcmd = θ*FF + 0.5·θmax·πtray(o). Speed is fully learned. This keeps the hardware safe early in training.
  • Curriculum from a fixed route without pedestrians to randomized maps with rising pedestrian probability. The level increases automatically when the success rate exceeds 80%.
  • Domain randomization of servo delay, time constant and rate; cup mass, h and r; friction μ; motor lag; IMU noise.
  • Optional DQN with a discretized action set, as a link to the Q-learning covered in class.
  • Budget: about one million environment steps per run, 3 seeds per method.

5. Planned work, experiments and expected results

All methods run on the same maps and event schedules. We report success rate, tip rate, collision rate and mean time of successful episodes (so that episodes that fail early do not look fast).

  1. Active vs. fixed tray at fixed speed profiles: tip rate as braking and cornering intensity increase.
  2. Joint speed and tray control: constant speed vs. MPC vs. RL vs. residual RL on randomized hotel maps with stochastic pedestrians.
  3. Ablations: acceleration preview on/off; recurrent vs. feed-forward policy; curriculum vs. training directly on random maps.
  4. Hardware: fixed tray vs. feedforward vs. learned policy on a taped-out corridor with doors and wooden bumps; tipping detected with the dual-IMU method.

Expected results. The active tray should allow noticeably harder braking and cornering before the cup tips. Conservative MPC should be safe but slow; RL should keep speed on straight, visible corridors and slow down only before doors and blind corners, reaching shorter delivery times at a similar failure rate. The preview feedforward is a strong tray baseline, so RL's main gain is expected to come from speed decisions under partial observability.
