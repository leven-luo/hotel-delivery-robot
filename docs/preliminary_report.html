Preliminary Report — Hotel Delivery Robot with an Active Two-Axis Tray

Project 5 · Reinforcement Learning and Optimal Control for Autonomous Systems I · November 2026

1. GitHub repository

https://github.com/leven-luo/hotel-delivery-robot (public)

2. Project description

We build a hotel delivery robot that carries a tall cup on an actively tilting two-axis tray and must reach a guest room from the lobby as fast as possible without tipping the cup or colliding with people. The policy jointly controls forward speed, tray pitch and tray roll. Tilting the tray toward the effective gravity vector g − a lets the robot brake and turn harder than a fixed tray would allow.

The hotel map (corridors, doors, turns, intersections) is known, but whether a person steps out of a door or a blind corner is not: such events occur with location-dependent probabilities, and the policy only receives what its sensors could observe at that moment. Bumps and slippery patches add disturbances. The task combines optimal control (speed and tray commands jointly determine travel time and tipping risk along the whole trajectory) with reinforcement learning (acting under partial observability, e.g. learning where to slow down before doors without being told whether someone is behind them).

3. Tools

  • Simulator (our own, Python/NumPy): car longitudinal and lateral dynamics with motor lag; servo model (delay, first-order lag, rate and angle limits); chassis tilt; a cup model on the tray. Wrapped as a Gymnasium environment so the same code serves the classical controllers and RL.
  • Learning: Stable-Baselines3 (SAC, PPO) on PyTorch; sb3-contrib for recurrent PPO. Optimal control: NumPy (LQR, feedforward) and CasADi (MPC).
  • Hardware (planned): Waveshare UGV02 chassis (ESP32, 9-axis IMU, wheel encoders, 4 kg payload); a 3D-printed two-axis gimbal (fixed bracket, outer frame, food tray) with a DS3218 servo on one side of each axis and a 625ZZ bearing on the other; an ESP32 tray controller running the policy at 100 Hz; IMUs on the tray and inside the cup (ESP-NOW link) to detect tipping; a forward ToF sensor for bumps.

4. Algorithms

Optimal-control baselines. (i) Fixed tray with a constant or hand-tuned speed profile (lower bound). (ii) Gravity-aligned feedforward: tilt the tray to atan(a/g), using the planner's previewed acceleration to compensate servo lag. (iii) LQR for tray tilt around the linearized cup-on-tray dynamics. (iv) MPC over speed and tray angles that minimizes travel time subject to a no-tip constraint (relative acceleration below the cup's tipping threshold) and a stopping-distance constraint at every door and blind corner assuming a pedestrian may appear — safe but conservative.

Reinforcement learning. SAC/PPO with action = (speed command, tray pitch, tray roll). Reward: −c per time step, +R on arrival, −Rfail and episode end on tipping or collision. Partial observability is handled with a stacked observation history or a recurrent policy; the policy sees only map features and sensor readings, never the hidden event schedule. Residual RL learns a correction on top of the feedforward tray command (safer on hardware). A curriculum moves from a fixed route to randomized maps and events, with domain randomization of servo delay, cup mass and center-of-mass height, friction and sensor noise.

5. Planned work, experiments and expected results

All methods run on the same maps and event schedules. We report success rate, tip rate, collision rate and mean time of successful episodes (so that episodes that fail early do not look fast).

  1. Active vs. fixed tray at fixed speed profiles: tip rate as braking and cornering intensity increase.
  2. Joint speed and tray control: constant speed vs. MPC vs. RL vs. residual RL on randomized hotel maps with stochastic pedestrians.
  3. Ablations: acceleration preview on/off; recurrent vs. feed-forward policy; curriculum vs. training directly on random maps.
  4. Hardware: fixed tray vs. feedforward vs. learned policy on a taped-out corridor with doors and wooden bumps; tipping detected with the dual-IMU method.

Expected results. The active tray should allow noticeably harder braking and cornering before the cup tips. Conservative MPC should be safe but slow; RL should keep speed on straight, visible corridors and slow down only before doors and blind corners, reaching shorter delivery times at a similar failure rate. The preview feedforward is a strong tray baseline, so RL's main gain is expected to come from speed decisions under partial observability.

  • Design decisions since v0: the failure metric changed from spilled water to cup tipping (weighing during motion is corrupted by bumps and braking): a tall cup carries an IMU, and a tip is declared when its tilt relative to the tray IMU exceeds a calibrated threshold for a minimum duration. The task was extended from tray-only control to joint speed and tray control on a hotel map; simulator v0 is being extended accordingly.
  • Mechanical design and hardware selection: 160 × 160 mm tray, ±15° per axis, servo-plus-bearing joint design, and a priced parts list.
